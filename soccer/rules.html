📋 Rules

The Laws of the Game in plain English

The Basics

  • Two teams of 11 players each (including one goalkeeper). A match can't start or continue if a team has fewer than 7.
  • A full match is two 45-minute halves with a half-time break. The referee adds stoppage time for injuries, substitutions, and time-wasting.
  • Score by getting the whole ball over the goal line between the posts and under the crossbar. The team with more goals wins; a tie is a draw unless the competition needs extra time or penalties.
  • Only the goalkeeper may handle the ball — and only inside their own penalty area.
  • Adult matches use a size 5 ball. Youth leagues use smaller balls (size 3 or 4) and smaller teams, fields, and goals.
  • Shin guards are required. Jewelry is not allowed.

Ball In & Out of Play

  • The ball is out only when the whole ball has crossed the touchline or goal line — on the ground or in the air. If any part of it is on the line, it's still in.
  • Over the touchline (side) → a throw-in to the team that didn't touch it last.
  • Over the goal line (end), last touched by the attackers → a goal kick. Last touched by the defenders → a corner kick.
  • The ball stays in play if it bounces off the referee, a post, the crossbar, or a corner flag and stays on the field.

Offside

  • You're in an offside position if, when a teammate plays the ball to you, you're in the opponents' half and closer to their goal line than both the ball and the second-to-last defender (the goalkeeper usually counts as one).
  • Being level with the second-to-last defender — or level with the ball — is onside.
  • Being in an offside position isn't an offence by itself. It's only called if you become involved in active play: playing the ball, blocking the keeper's view, or challenging an opponent for it.
  • You can't be offside in your own half, or directly from a throw-in, goal kick, or corner kick.
  • What counts is where you were when the ball was played, not where you are when you receive it.
  • Penalty: an indirect free kick to the defending team from where the offence happened.

Fouls & Misconduct

  • Direct free kick fouls (you can score straight from the kick): kicking, tripping, pushing, charging, striking, or jumping at an opponent carelessly, recklessly, or with excessive force; holding; tackling the player instead of the ball; and deliberate handball.
  • A direct-free-kick foul by a defender inside their own penalty area is a penalty kick.
  • Indirect free kick offences (someone else must touch it before a goal counts): dangerous play (e.g. a high kick near someone's head), impeding an opponent without contact, offside, and certain goalkeeper offences.
  • Advantage: if stopping play would hurt the team that was fouled, the referee may wave "play on" — and can still show a card at the next stoppage.
Yellow card — a caution: reckless fouls, dissent, delaying the restart, not retreating 10 yards, unsporting behavior.
Red card — sent off, team plays short: serious foul play, violent conduct, spitting, denying an obvious goal-scoring chance, abusive language, or a second yellow.

Handball

  • The "arm" starts at the bottom of the armpit — the shoulder is fair game.
  • It's handball if you deliberately touch the ball with your hand or arm, or if your arm makes your body unnaturally bigger and the ball hits it.
  • An attacker can't score directly with their hand or arm, even accidentally.
  • A ball that hits an arm held close to the body, or an arm supporting a player as they fall, usually is not handball.

Free Kicks & Penalties

  • On any free kick, opponents must stand at least 10 yards (9.15 m) away until the ball is kicked and clearly moves.
  • On a direct free kick, a defensive wall of three or more players means attackers must stay at least 1 yard from the wall.
  • A penalty kick is taken from the spot 12 yards (11 m) from goal. Everyone except the kicker and keeper stays outside the box and the arc.
  • On a penalty, the goalkeeper must stay on the goal line between the posts until the kick, with at least part of one foot touching or in line with the line.
  • The kicker can't touch the ball again until another player touches it — no tapping in your own rebound off the post.

Throw-ins, Goal Kicks & Corners

  • Throw-in: face the field, use both hands, deliver from behind and over your head, with part of each foot on the touchline or the ground outside it. A bad throw goes to the other team.
  • You can't score directly from a throw-in, and the thrower can't touch it again until someone else does.
  • Goal kick: taken from anywhere in the goal area. The ball is in play as soon as it's kicked and clearly moves — it doesn't have to leave the penalty area.
  • Corner kick: taken from inside the corner arc nearest where the ball went out. You can score directly from a corner.
  • Kick-off: starts each half and restarts play after a goal. The ball goes in any direction, and you can score directly from it.

The Goalkeeper

  • The keeper may handle the ball only inside their own penalty area. Outside it, they're a field player.
  • The keeper can't pick up a ball deliberately kicked to them by a teammate (a "back-pass"), or one thrown directly to them from a teammate's throw-in. Heading or chesting it back is fine.
  • Once the keeper has the ball in their hands, they have 8 seconds to release it. Holding it longer gives the other team a corner kick.
  • Opponents can't challenge a keeper who has control of the ball in their hands.
