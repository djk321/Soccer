📖 Helpful Terms

Soccer vocabulary you need to know

PitchThe playing field. Also called the park or, in the U.S., simply the field
TouchlineThe long sideline. Ball fully over it = a throw-in
Box (Penalty Area)The large rectangle in front of each goal. The keeper can handle the ball here; defensive fouls here mean a penalty kick
Six-Yard BoxThe small rectangle right in front of goal (the goal area), where goal kicks are taken
OffsideBeing past the second-to-last defender and the ball when a teammate passes to you, then getting involved in play
Set PieceA planned play from a restart — corner kicks, free kicks, and sometimes long throw-ins
Through BallA pass played into space behind the defense for a teammate to run onto
One-Two (Give-and-Go)Pass to a teammate, sprint past your defender, and get it right back. Also called a wall pass
CrossA pass from a wide area into the box, usually aimed for a teammate to head or volley
Near Post / Far PostThe goalpost closest to the ball / the one farthest away. "Back post" means far post
VolleyStriking the ball before it touches the ground. A half-volley is struck just as it bounces
First TouchHow you control the ball when it first reaches you — a good one sets up your next move
NutmegPlaying the ball through an opponent's legs and collecting it on the other side
OverlapA teammate (often a fullback) running around the outside of the player with the ball to create a 2-v-1
Switch the PlayA long pass from one side of the field to the other to attack where the defense is thin
PressingClosing down opponents quickly as a team to win the ball back high up the field
CounterattackAttacking fast right after winning the ball, before the other team can get back into shape
JockeyDefending by staying on your feet, side-on, slowing the attacker down instead of diving in
Man On!Warning shout to a teammate that an opponent is right behind them
Clean SheetA game in which a team (and its keeper) concedes no goals. Also called a shutout
Hat TrickThree goals by one player in one game. Two goals is a brace
Stoppage TimeMinutes added at the end of each half for time lost to injuries, subs, and delays. Also called injury or added time
RondoA keep-away passing drill: a circle of players passing around one or two defenders in the middle
FutsalAn indoor 5-a-side version of soccer with a smaller, heavier, low-bounce ball
Walking SoccerA slower version for older or returning players: no running, no slide tackles, and usually no ball above head height
Bicycle KickAn acrobatic overhead kick, with the player's back to goal and body in the air
