🤝 Fair Play

Play hard, play fair, play safe

The Referee
🟨
Respect the Ref — Even When They're Wrong
▼
Referees miss things; so do players. Arguing a call almost never changes it — and in organized play, dissent earns a yellow card. In youth and rec leagues the ref is often a teenager or a volunteer. If you have a genuine question, let your captain ask calmly at a stoppage. Then move on to the next play.
🙋
No Ref? Call Your Own Fouls
▼
In pickup games, players referee themselves. If you foul someone, say so and give them the free kick. Common examples:
  • Clipping someone's heels from behind
  • The ball hitting your hand or arm
  • Knowing the ball went off you before it went out
Owning up builds trust. Never try to win by sneaking past the rules.
Safety
🦵
Play the Ball, Not the Player
▼
Tackle to win the ball, never to hurt. No studs-up challenges, no tackles from behind, and keep your elbows down in the air. In casual and older-adult games, skip slide tackles entirely — most rec and walking-soccer leagues ban them anyway. Nobody wants to miss work (or a grandchild's birthday) because of a pickup game.
🩹
Kick It Out for an Injury — and Give It Back
▼
If a player is down and hurt, the team with the ball kicks it out of play so they can be helped. When play restarts, the other team gives possession back — usually by throwing or kicking it to the team that put it out. It's one of soccer's oldest unwritten rules, and everyone notices who honors it.
Teammates
🏅
Never Get Mad at a Teammate
▼
When a teammate misplaces a pass or lets in a goal, they already feel bad. Don't throw up your arms or yell. The best things you can say: "Unlucky," "Next one," "Keep going." A clap and a thumbs-up does more than any lecture. Confident teammates make better decisions.
🔁
Pass the Ball — Share the Game
▼
Dribbling is fun, but nobody enjoys playing with a ball hog. Look for the open teammate, especially the ones who haven't touched it in a while. In pickup, take your turn in goal and on defense — rotating keepers is standard etiquette.
📣
Talk — Helpfully
▼
Good communication wins games: "Man on!" (someone's behind you), "Time!" (you have space), "Keeper's!" and "Away!" (clear it). Keep it to short, useful information — not a running critique of how your teammates are playing. Save the coaching for after the game, and only if they ask.
Sportsmanship
🎭
Don't Dive, Don't Waste Time
▼
Diving (faking a foul) and rolling around to fool the ref is a yellow card in the Laws of the Game — and a reputation-killer in a pickup game. Same goes for stalling: slow-walking to take throw-ins or kicking the ball away after a whistle. Win by playing better.
😤
Don't Be a Sore Winner or a Sore Loser
▼
Sore winner: taunting, over-the-top celebrations in a lopsided game, running up the score against kids or beginners. Celebrate with your team, not at the other one.

Sore loser: blaming the ref, the field, or your teammates. Shake every hand after the match — opponents and referee — and tell them "good game." Mean it.
On the Sideline
👨‍👩‍👧
Cheer Effort, Let the Coach Coach
▼
Parents and grandparents are a team's best fans. Cheer for good effort and good plays by both teams, not just goals. Don't shout instructions ("Shoot!" "Pass!") — kids can't listen to the coach and the sideline at the same time, and conflicting advice freezes them. Never yell at the referee; youth leagues lose refs every year because of sideline abuse.
🚗
The Ride Home Matters
▼
Skip the post-game analysis in the car. The best thing to say to a young player after a game, win or lose: "I love watching you play." Let them bring up the game if they want to talk about it.
